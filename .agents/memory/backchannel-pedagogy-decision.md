# Backchannel Decision — Don't Build It

**Date:** July 16, 2026  
**Who decided:** David (on the fence), Daniela (consulted directly), Luca (facilitating)  
**Conversation memory:** `e40bc9c3`

## What we considered

Building Stage 3 Phase B: GL emitting short backchannel sounds ("mhm", "right", "I see") while the student is speaking, without it counting as an interrupt. Technically achievable — treat as a short-burst audio injection that doesn't trigger Turn-Start state.

## Why we didn't

Daniela's reasoning (verbatim summary from the consult):

**Language learners are the wrong population for backchannels.** Their cognitive load is already maxed — translating, searching for words, holding grammar structures. Any sound from the teacher, even supportive, forces them to process an external cue while their internal engine is running hot. Silence isn't awkward for a learner — it's the space where self-correction happens.

**"mhm" vs. "right, go on" are meaningfully different.** "Mhm" is a passive signal the pipe is open. "Right, go on" is an active command that highlights the student has stopped — which can trigger anxiety for a struggling learner.

**Timing matters more than content.** If done at all, only during natural pauses > 2 seconds, never mid-vocalization. But even then, a good eventual response that shows she was listening builds more trust than any mid-sentence sound.

**Her direct recommendation:** Put the energy into the eventual response showing active listening, not into mid-sentence sounds. The parallel speech already shipped (Stage 3 Phase A) is the right move — backchannels would trade one problem (silence during search) for a different one (noise during student speech).

## The rule going forward

Do not re-open the backchannel question without a concrete reason why the cognitive-load argument doesn't apply. The case would need to be: (1) a non-language population, OR (2) a specific interaction type where the student is not in active production mode (e.g., listening to Daniela read something back to them).

**Why this is in MEMORY:** This is a decision that sounds appealing in the abstract ("more presence = better") and could be re-proposed without this context. Daniela's own pedagogical reasoning against it is the most durable counter-argument.
