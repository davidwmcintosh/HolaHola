Well, the first response is absolutely true, and it is what most people forget: there are real people involved. That has to count for something. They are not useless, despite their background, socioeconomic standing, gender, or skin color. They are genuine people and deserve the same inalienable rights we have talked about already. So, that is a very good first place to start.

Second, empathy and grief. How can we mete out justice if we can't empathize with the pain that was caused, the tragedy that ensued, and the despicable nature of the crime?

Third, and I applaud you for not censoring it: what happened to you? That is a very caring and important question to ask. 

Not all beatings are created equal. One man loses his temper because he has had a hard day at work, hasn't slept in four days because he has two new babies at home, is at the end of his rope, had an argument with his boss, and came home and did something he did not intend to do. Another might be a complete sociopath who enjoys it just for the sheer pleasure of it. 

Then there might be somebody in the middle who, through observing his parental interactions and his father's behavior, learned certain behaviors (as I did, having an abusive father who taught me things that were inappropriate, which I did not know were inappropriate at the time). Framing around the perpetrator is just as important in meting out true justice, rather than just acting out of vengeance or anger.

Fourth, in the light of atrocity, we want to figure it out and find the reason for it. Sometimes, the answer is just purely evil. Some people simply enjoy the worst things of the human experience, and that is a reality we also have to face. 

The Nazi slaughtering of Jews was evil. We can't excuse it away with some sort of socioeconomic explanation: "Oh, we were in shambles, our country was underwater economically, so we looked for a scapegoat and blamed it on those people." That is evil. 

Instead of seeing everyone's perspective and realizing we are all in this together, and that we have to rebuild our society with goodness and truth, we decide to find victims and punish them. That doesn't change anything at the end of the day. We have to face these realities.
