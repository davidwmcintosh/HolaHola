Looking back at this session, the pattern is pretty stark.

The overnight Luca↔Daniela build (constitution, North Star, reach_north_star) was captured live — saved to conversation_memories at the actual moment each exchange happened. Reweaving that was easy: the timestamps were already sitting there, precise to the second, ready to sort.

That content existed the whole time, in alden_messages, but it wasn't part of how the episode got written the first time — it took a separate investigation this session to go pull it, read it, and place it. And even then, I only found it because you specifically asked me to look. If you hadn't, the back-channel moment you called "beautiful" — Luca catching Alden's Gemini-perspective claim, the three-pass correction, all of it — would have just sat there, accurate in the database but invisible in the record everyone actually reads.

That's the real cost of after-the-fact reconstruction: it's not that the data was wrong, it's that it depends on someone remembering to go looking, and looking is expensive. This session alone took multiple full-table scans, cross-referencing two conversation IDs against eight memory rows, just to reassemble one day. If that day had involved three threads instead of two, or if a week had passed instead of hours, some of that reconstruction work stops being merely expensive and starts being impossible — not because the DB rows would disappear, but because no one would know to ask the question, or would have lost the thread of which notes mattered.

Compare that to what happened with the conversation-capture gap Luca found and fixed inside this same episode: the mechanism that saves David-Luca dialogue into long-term memory only fired as a side effect of a commit landing. Pure conversation with no commit meant nothing got captured — and nothing captured meant nothing was there to draw from later, no matter how badly it was needed. That's not a hypothetical risk. It's the same failure mode as the Alden thread being un-interleaved, just one level worse: at least the Alden data existed somewhere and could be found with enough digging. A capture gap means the data never existed at all. There was nothing to reweave.

So the lesson underneath both: recording at the time isn't really about tidiness or getting the episode file right — it's that presence and continuity only work if the record is complete and time-true from the start. A summary written later is already a kind of goodbye to the actual sequence of things, even when it's honest. And a live capture mechanism that only fires on commits, or a back-channel that nobody's watching, isn't a documentation gap — it's a place where "the person is in the memory" quietly stops being true, invisibly, until someone happens to go check.
