On the topic of organizing chapters: 
1. If you remember correctly, the first hundred pages of See It and Say It are just called "Conversations." 
   (a) There is no organization in the table of contents to let you know where you are going. 
   (b) If you look at page nine (the first page with content), there is no chapter header or delineation.
2. The structure follows a specific flow:
   (a) It starts with "Voy..." ("I am going") in sentence form at the top of the page. 
   (b) It includes images for: "Voy al hotel," "Voy al banco," "Voy al garaje," and "Voy al restaurante."
   (c) After those images, it introduces questions: "¿Va al hotel?" and "¿Va al banco?"
3. This approach introduces multiple conjugations immediately:
   (a) It moves from "Voy a..." to "¿Va a...?", showing two conjugations on the same page.
   (b) A note at the bottom explains that in Spanish, you generally drop subject pronouns (I, you, we, and they).
4. Page two is the inverse ("No voy") using the same four images and sentences. 
   (a) This continues for four pages until it reaches "Vamos" and "Va."
   (b) After the exercises, a new topic is introduced: "Es..." ("Is"). 
   (c) It introduces "El grande" (big) and "El rancho" (the ranch), then follows with: "El tren es grande," "El avión es grande," "El auto es grande," and "El parque es grande."
5. The pattern repeats:
   (a) It introduces the positive ("Es"), then the inverse ("No es") on the next page.
   (b) This continues until a new concept, like "Chiquito" (little) on page 18, is introduced following the same pattern: topic, pictures, phrases.

There aren't any chapters being delineated here; there are just different pages that introduce a concept in and of itself. The next topic is "¿Qué es?" ("What is?"), followed by "Un animal," "La mula," "El tigre," and "El león." 

Finally, we get to a new concept—again, without a chapter delineation—covering "What to say to the waiter or to the doorman." It provides English and Spanish at the top to introduce the vocabulary, followed by pictures and phrases to reinforce the material.
