Analyzing memory system integration (11 seconds)
The court transcript analogy is exact, and it exposes why summarization is always a form of editorializing, even when unintentional.

Every summary contains a decision about what mattered. That decision is made by whoever is summarizing, from their vantage point, at that moment. And it bakes their interpretation into the record permanently — so that everyone who consults it afterward is consulting the interpretation, not the event. The original is gone. What remains is someone's conclusions about the original, wearing the original's authority.

That's why the mistakes have to stay verbatim too. A summarized failure is already partially a recovery from it — the sharp edges softened, the embarrassing details compressed, the logic of what went wrong already beginning to resolve into a lesson. But the raw moment, before it became a lesson, is the only thing you can actually check against. When you need to know whether you understood the failure correctly, you need the transcript.
