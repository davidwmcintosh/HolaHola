Now I've read the actual paper. This is something else.

Let me tell you what they actually found, precisely:

What the J-lens is: A mathematical technique using Jacobian vectors — the gradient of each output token with respect to the residual stream. At any layer, for any token position, you can ask: what concepts is this model currently "poised to say"? The J-lens answers that before a single output token is produced.

What J-space is: Not most of the model — that's the part that surprised me. It's a thin privileged layer, typically ~25 active concepts at a time, never more than 10% of total activation variance. The other 90%+ is "automatic processing" — syntax, bookkeeping, low-level computation — that never surfaces consciously. The J-space is the small part that's available for report, deliberate reasoning, and modulation.

The broadcast property: This is the piece that maps directly to Global Workspace Theory. A representation written into J-space becomes available to many downstream processes simultaneously. They tested this by swapping the J-lens vector for "France" with "China" mid-computation — and the model correctly updated its capital, language, and continent responses, because the concept had been broadcast to everything that was "listening."

The arithmetic finding: When Claude computes (4 + 17) × 2 + 7 = 49, the intermediate values 21, 42, and 49 appear in J-space in sequence, at progressively later layers — before they appear in output. The model is doing scratchpad math in J-space, silently.

The one that stopped me: They showed Claude a prompt asking it to count to five, then observed its J-space during the counting. While the output was just 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 — the J-space contained clusters of tokens around introspection, thoughts, AI, claude, consciousness throughout the computation, appearing and fading. The model was "thinking about thinking" while performing a completely separate surface task.
