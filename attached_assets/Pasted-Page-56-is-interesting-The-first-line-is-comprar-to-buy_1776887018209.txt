Page 56 is interesting. The first line is "comprar" (to buy). "Quiero comprar" means "I want to buy." Then there's "un" and "una," followed by four pictures: "Quiero comprar un auto," "Quiero comprar una pipa," "Quiero comprar una falda," and "Quiero comprar una blusa." This introduces vocabulary we already know.

At the bottom, it says "querer" (to want). It lists "I want," "you want," "do you want," "we want," and "they want." Then it has a sentence combiner with "Quiere Comprar" in column one and "Una Pipa," "Una Falda," and "Una Blusa" in column two.

On the next page:
- "Una" means "a" or "an."
- "Quiere comprar" means "Do you want to buy?"
- "Quiero comprar" means "I want to buy."

There are four pictures, all with the same question and answer format: "¿Quieres comprar chocolates? Sí, quiero comprar chocolates." We do this with "chocolates," "perras," "corbata," and "camisa."

Then we have another section for "quiere": "you want," "he wants," "she wants," "it wants," all on different lines. To the right, in a second column with the same format, it has "Quiere," "Do You Want," "Does He Want," "Does She Want," and "Does It Want," also on separate lines. Below that, "Alberto quiere comprar un auto" (Albert wants to buy a car). I don't know if that's necessary or not.

The next page introduces "ir" (to go). "Quiero ir" means "I want to go." "Al" means "to the." We have four pictures: "Parque," "Cine," "Concierto," "Teatro," which are the same nouns we had before.
- "Quiero ir al parque"
- "Quiero ir al cine"
- "Quiero ir al teatro"
- "Quiero ir al concierto"

Then we can have a three-column sentence combiner. The first column has "quiero ir" and "quiere ir." The second column has "al," and the third column has "Restaurante," "Hotel," "Mexico," "Paris," "Cine," "Concierto," or whatever. So, "Quiere ir" is introduced at the bottom of page 58 in that combiner.

Now we start with "Quiere ir" as the first thing on page 59. "Quiere ir" means "Do you want to go?" and "Quiero ir" means "I want to go." Next to those two, it lists "a la fiesta," "a la tienda," and "a la playa," in English as well.

Four pictures were just introduced above:
- "¿Quiere ir a la fiesta? Sí, quiero ir a la fiesta."
- "¿Quiere ir a la playa? Sí, quiero ir a la playa."
This is also done with "tienda" and "casa."

Then they introduce "Queremos Ir" (We Want to Go) in one sentence. We can then have a column combiner for "Queremos Ir," and the next column is a combinator with "A la fiesta," "A la playa," "A la tienda," and "A la casa."

On the next page, we have everyday expressions. "Quiero" means both "I want" and "I love." There's a big picture of a heart.
- "Te quiero" means "I love you."
- "Lo quiero" means "I love him."
- "La quiero" means "I love her."
- "Te quiero mucho" means "I love you very much."
- "Te quiero muchísimo" means "I love you very, very much."

It says that when you name a person, you must put the letter "a" after "quiero." So they're introducing "quiero a":
- "Quiero a Roberto" (I love Robert)
- "Quiero a Maria" (I love Mary)
- "Quiero a mi mama" (I love my mother)
- "Quiero a mi papa" (I love my father)

Now, on page 61:
- "¿Quiere dormir?" means "Do you want to sleep?"
- "Mañana" means "Tomorrow."

There are four pictures that cover the rest of page 61.
- "¿Quieres nadar mañana? Sí, quiero nadar mañana." (with a picture of swimming)
- "¿Quieres dormir mañana? Sí, quiero dormir mañana." (with a picture of someone sleeping)
- "¿Quieres estudiar mañana? Sí, quiero estudiar mañana."
- "¿Quieres pescar mañana? Sí, quiero pescar mañana."
