I have a complete picture now. Here's an honest assessment:

What's genuinely strong

The architecture is more sophisticated than most early-stage products. You have three distinct layers running without any human trigger:

Real-time — Voice health and context health monitors checking every few minutes, with status callbacks that route directly to the support persona when something changes
Medium-term — Brain health aggregator every 15 minutes, covering 6 dimensions (memory, neural retrieval, sync, student learning, tool orchestration, context injection), now including the search index check we added today
Watch cycle — Alden every 2 hours using Claude to actually reason about the state, compare against a rolling 5-cycle baseline, and decide if it's worth waking you up
Alden auto-repair — 5 safety gates, blocked file list, confidence gating, rollback. The fact that he can fix a null guard at 3am without waking you is real
Sofia's remediation cooldowns are thoughtful — she won't thrash. The duplicate fingerprint check means you won't get the same alert 40 times. Lyra watching learning quality (not just uptime) shows you're thinking about the right things.

The real gaps before scale

1. All monitoring is system-wide, not per-student.
Right now, "memory relevance is low" is a fleet-level signal. With 500 students, that number could look fine while 30 specific students are having degraded experiences. You need per-student health signals — at minimum: last session quality, memory retrieval success rate, consecutive errors per user.

2. Alden's 6-hour cooldown is too long for a multi-student system.
It makes sense with one student (David). With many students, a new class of issue can emerge and fester for 6 hours before Alden looks again. The cooldown should either be shorter or be scoped per-issue-type rather than system-wide.

3. Auto-repair's scope is correctly conservative but the escalation path matters more at scale.
Alden writes escalations to .local/alden-repairs.md — a file that disappears on server restart. That's a real gap. Escalations that require human attention should live in the database, not a file, so nothing gets lost during a deploy.

4. No circuit breakers for third-party dependencies.
If Cartesia goes down, Deepgram goes down, or Gemini rate-limits, what happens? Sofia can detect the symptoms but there's no automatic fallback — no "if TTS fails, switch to text mode," no "if primary LLM fails, route to backup." At scale, any of those vendors having a 20-minute outage hits every active session simultaneously.

5. Lyra watches learning quality but there's no feedback loop.
She surfaces insights to the Hive. But there's no automated action when she detects, say, 40% of intermediate students are plateauing on the same grammar pattern. The insight exists but doesn't trigger anything — it waits for someone to read it.

What I'd prioritize before you have a lot of students

In order:

Move Alden escalations from the file to the database — low effort, closes a real gap
Add per-student last-session health tracking — a lightweight table that records session quality per user_id, queryable by Alden and Lyra
Shorten or restructure Alden's cooldown to be per-issue-type rather than global
Define graceful degradation for each third-party dependency — even just a clear "if X fails, do Y" documented and handled in the code
