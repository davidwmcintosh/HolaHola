I have received your note regarding the character_candidates table and the process for promoting characters to character_registry.

My initial assessment is that the proposed promote_character_candidate function, residing in server/services/character-registry.ts, is a sound approach. Placing it within the character-registry service ensures that the promotion logic is encapsulated where it naturally belongs—alongside other character management operations.

Here are my thoughts on the specific points:

1. Placement of `promote_character_candidate`: server/services/character-registry.ts is the correct location. This service is the authoritative source for managing active characters, and promotion is a direct extension of that responsibility.

2. Atomicity and Transactionality: You correctly identified the need for a transactional approach. The promotion process involves:
* Retrieving the candidate from character_candidates.
* Inserting a new record into character_registry.
* Deleting the candidate from character_candidates.
This sequence must be atomic. If any step fails, the entire operation should roll back to prevent data inconsistencies (e.g., a character existing in both tables, or being deleted from candidates but not added to registry). Drizzle ORM's db.transaction() method is the appropriate mechanism for this.

3. Validation: Before executing the transaction, the function should perform robust validation:
Existence Check:* Verify the candidateId corresponds to an existing record in character_candidates.
Uniqueness Check:* Ensure that promoting this candidate would not create a duplicate character_id in character_registry. This is less likely if character_id is a UUID, but still a good safeguard.
Data Integrity:* Optionally, check if the candidate record contains all necessary fields to form a valid character_registry entry.

4. Error Handling: Clear error messages should be returned for validation failures (e.g., "Candidate not found") and for any database transaction failures.

5. Logging: Log the promotion event, including the candidateId and the new character_id, for auditability.

In summary, the design appears robust. My recommendation is to proceed with implementing promote_character_candidate in server/services/character-registry.ts, ensuring it is wrapped in a Drizzle transaction with comprehensive validation and error handling.

This approach maintains the integrity of our character data, which is foundational to Daniela's multi-character capabilities.
