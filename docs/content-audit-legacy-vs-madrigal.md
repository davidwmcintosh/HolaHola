# Content Audit: Legacy Chapters vs. Madrigal Backbone
**Date:** April 2026  
**Purpose:** Distinguish between chapters built using traditional textbook conventions ("legacy") and chapters built directly from Madrigal's method ("Madrigal backbone"). Document duplication, note assets that can be repurposed, and guide decisions about what to keep, retire, or integrate.

---

## Background

The Spanish 1 course was built in two phases:

**Phase 1 — Legacy chapters (roughly Ch. 1–22, 24–27)**  
Created using traditional textbook topic sequencing (school, hobbies, food, weather, travel). Topics were selected because they appear in conventional Spanish 1 curricula, not because Madrigal covers them. These chapters may have good vocabulary lists, images, and interactive assets — but their pedagogical framing does not match Madrigal's method.

**Phase 2 — Madrigal backbone (Ch. 23, 28–45)**  
Built directly from Madrigal's *See It and Say It in Spanish* textbook. Chapter content, sentence pairs, Q&A patterns, and vocabulary are sourced from the book. These chapters use the GustUnit, HayUnit, VerbUnit, and PretUnit renderers and follow Madrigal's communicative-first logic. This is the authoritative spine of the course.

**Content law:** Spanish content (sentences, paradigms, Q&A pairs) must come from Madrigal, never generated by AI. This applies to both phases — but legacy chapters were sometimes built before this rule was formalized.

## Identified Duplications

### 1. Voy a — Two chapters ⚠️
- **Ch. 30** "I'm Going To: Voy a + Infinitive" — Madrigal chapter, early version
- **Ch. 40** "Voy a: I Am Going To" — GustUnit, full Madrigal development

These cover the same topic with different depth. Ch. 40 is the definitive Madrigal version with the full Q&A, sentence combiner, and GustUnit rendering. Ch. 30 predates the GustUnit renderer.

**Decision pending:** Determine whether Ch. 30 should be retired, repurposed as an introduction, or left as a lighter precursor to Ch. 40.

### 2. Gustar — Three chapters ⚠️
- **Ch. 11** (School Subjects) — Introduces gustar in the context of subjects
- **Ch. 14** (Hobbies) — Uses me gusta for activities
- **Ch. 37** (Gustar: Me gusta/gustan) — Full Madrigal gustar chapter

Chs. 11 and 14 introduce gustar before the dedicated chapter. This is not necessarily a problem (spiral learning), but the legacy chapters' sourcing is not Madrigal — the sentences and patterns may not match the book's framing.

**Decision pending:** Whether Chs. 11 and 14 function as productive previews or create confusion by teaching gustar in a non-Madrigal frame before Ch. 37.

### 3. Ser — Two chapters ⚠️
- **Ch. 9** "Ser — Describing" — Legacy chapter (adjectives, personality, appearance)
- **Ch. 33** "Ser: Nature of Things" — Madrigal chapter (el/la/los/las, plurals, es/son + adjectives)

These cover different aspects of ser, so there is less direct duplication than the Voy a case. However, the framing may conflict — Ch. 9 teaches ser as a describing word in a traditional way, while Ch. 33 follows Madrigal's specific treatment.

### 4. Poder — Two chapters ⚠️
- **Ch. 15** (Sports & Abilities) — Introduces poder as "I can" for sports
- **Ch. 35** (Puedo ir) — Madrigal's HayUnit treatment of poder + ir

Similar to gustar: legacy introduction precedes the Madrigal chapter.

### 5. Present Tense AR (Ch. 12) — Philosophical tension ⚠️
This chapter teaches the full -o/-as/-a/-amos/-an paradigm. Madrigal's approach deliberately avoids this: instead of teaching the paradigm, Madrigal treats high-frequency present-tense forms (voy, tengo, quiero, puedo, como, vivo) as vocabulary items learned individually.

Teaching the -AR paradigm before Madrigal's method kicks in may create two competing mental models in the student: "I apply endings to stems" vs. "I know these forms as words."

**Decision pending:** Whether to retire Ch. 12, reframe it as optional enrichment, or keep it as a grammar reference that complements rather than drives the Madrigal flow.

## Recommendations (Documentation Only — No Build Actions)

1. **Madrigal backbone takes precedence.** When a Madrigal chapter and a legacy chapter cover the same topic, the Madrigal chapter is the authoritative version.

2. **Legacy chapters serving unique topics keep their place.** Chs. 1–8 (social phrases, introductions, classroom survival, numbers, time, dates, family) have no Madrigal equivalent and are foundational. Chs. 17–18 (food/restaurant), 20–21 (colors/shopping), 24–27 (directions, travel, weather, hotel) similarly cover topics Madrigal doesn't address with a full chapter.

3. **Duplication review needed for:** Ch. 30 (Voy a), Ch. 9 vs. Ch. 33 (Ser), Ch. 11/14 (early gustar), Ch. 15 vs. Ch. 35 (poder). None of these need to be resolved immediately, but they should be audited before HoloHola is marketed to institutions requiring a clean chapter-by-chapter syllabus.

4. **Ch. 12 (Present Tense AR) is a tension point**, not a duplication. If Madrigal is the spine, this chapter teaches a different mental model. Worth flagging in any institution-facing syllabus as "grammar reference" rather than primary instruction.

5. **Regular preterite chapters (pages 122–141) are the highest-priority build gap.** These are firmly Madrigal content, not legacy. They require transcription.

6. **Physical appearance adjectives** can become a thin Madrigal chapter once sentences are transcribed; images already exist.
