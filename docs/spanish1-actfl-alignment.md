# Spanish 1 — ACTFL Alignment Audit
**Date:** April 2026  
**Purpose:** Map current chapter content against ACTFL Novice Can-Do Statements to identify gaps, confirm Spanish 1 scope, and draw a principled Spanish 1 / Spanish 2 boundary.

---

## The Madrigal Caveat

This audit deliberately does not treat ACTFL level descriptors as a *sequencing* rule. ACTFL describes what proficient speakers **can do** — it does not mandate the grammar order to get there.

Madrigal's method reaches Novice High communicative ability *faster* than traditional curricula by prioritizing high-frequency verbs as vocabulary items (voy, tengo, quiero, puedo) rather than drilling full present-tense paradigms. Students who finish the Madrigal-based Spanish 1 may legitimately test at Novice High or Intermediate Low on an ACTFL oral proficiency interview — which is a better outcome, not a mismatch.

ACTFL is used here as the **measurement framework** (what can students demonstrate?), not the **curriculum framework** (in what order?).

## Identified Gaps for Spanish 1 / Novice High

These are areas where ACTFL Novice High students should have competence but our current content is incomplete.

### Gap 1 — Regular AR/ER/IR Preterite Paradigm ⚠️ HIGH PRIORITY
**What's missing:** Pages 122–141 (chapters ~42–51 in the sequence) — the full regular preterite with all three verb endings.

Currently students can say *fui, tomé, compré* but cannot say *comí, escribí, viví, vendí, corrí* or apply the paradigm productively.

ACTFL NH requires: *"I can tell where I went and what I did."*  
Without the regular preterite paradigm, that coverage is thin.

**Recommendation:** Fill in pages 122–141 before finalizing the Spanish 1 scope. These are true Spanish 1 content.

### Gap 2 — Physical Descriptions / Appearance ⚠️ MEDIUM
**What's missing:** Adjectives for describing physical appearance — alto/bajo, delgado/gordo, rubio/moreno, joven/viejo, bonito/feo.

Ch. 9 (ser) covers personality and some appearance, but vocabulary for describing how people look is thin.

ACTFL NH: *"I can describe people and things."*

**Recommendation:** Add a short "Así es…" (describing people) cluster — can be folded into an existing chapter or added as a thin standalone.

### Gap 3 — Numbers Beyond 20 ⚠️ MEDIUM
**What's missing:** Numbers 21–100, hundreds, thousands. Prices above 20 can't be expressed.

Ch. 5 covers 0–20. Shopping (ch. 21) requires bigger numbers for prices, but students have no vocabulary for them.

**Recommendation:** A "Los Números II" chapter (21–100, then 100–1000) — important for Shopping, Travel, and Hotel chapters to function fully.

### Gap 4 — Health Vocabulary ⚠️ LOW–MEDIUM
**What's missing:** *Estoy enfermo/a, me duele la cabeza, tengo fiebre, el médico, la farmacia.*

ACTFL NH does not explicitly require health vocabulary, but it appears in most Novice High oral proficiency prompts and is a high-frequency real-world topic.

**Recommendation:** Consider adding as a late Spanish 1 chapter or early Spanish 2 chapter.

### Gap 5 — Clothing Vocabulary ⚠️ LOW
**What's missing:** Ch. 20 covers colors and implies clothing context, but specific clothing nouns (la camisa, los pantalones, el vestido, los zapatos) are light.

**Recommendation:** Extend Ch. 20 or add a brief "La Ropa" vocabulary cluster within Spanish 1.

## The Spanish 1 / Spanish 2 Boundary

### Spanish 1 ends at: Novice High

**Last chapter in Spanish 1:** Chapter 45 (Está: Limpio y Sucio) — adjective agreement with estar is a natural closer. The regular preterite gap (pages 122–141) should be added as Spanish 1 chapters before this boundary is finalized.

**Spanish 1 exit competencies (what students can do):**
- Greet, introduce, describe themselves and others
- Express likes, dislikes, preferences (gustar, gustaría, encantar)
- Talk about what they want, need, and have to do (querer, necesitar, tener que)
- Say where they are going and ask others (ir + destinations)
- Express future plans (voy a + infinitive)
- Narrate where they went and what they did (fui, tomé, compré, and regular -AR/-ER/-IR preterite)
- Handle simple transactions (restaurant, hotel, store)

## Spanish 2 — Opening Content (Intermediate Low)

The following chapters already built belong in Spanish 2:

| Ch | Name | Why Spanish 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 42 | ¿Qué Hizo? | Irregular preterites (hacer, oír, leer, poner, estar) — Intermediate Low |
| 43 | Tuvo: Did You Have? | Irregular preterites (tener, venir, poder) — Intermediate Low |
| 44 | Le: To Him, To Her | Indirect object pronoun — solidly Intermediate Low / Spanish 2 |

Spanish 2 then builds on these toward:
- Full irregular preterite paradigms
- Preterite vs. imperfect (ongoing vs. completed)
- Reflexive verbs
- Direct + indirect object pronouns together
- Commands (formal and informal)
- Introduction to subjunctive
